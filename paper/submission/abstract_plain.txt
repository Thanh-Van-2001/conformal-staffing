Objectives. Emergency medical service (EMS) staffing rules target an average delay and say nothing about how often a day goes badly. Conformal prediction has been used to put intervals around EMS demand forecasts, but not to calibrate the staffing decision itself. We tested whether calibrating an hourly ambulance staffing plan on recent days can meet a service target on nine days in ten, as conformal prediction promises for exchangeable data, and what this costs.

Methods. In this retrospective simulation study we used 4,696,716 public dispatch records from seven New York City and San Francisco service areas (weekdays, 2022–2025). A day met the target if at most 5% of calls waited for a free unit. Split conformal calibration (SCD) set the safety factor of a square-root staffing plan to a rank of the factors that recent days had needed. Over 287 out-of-sample 20-day windows, trace-driven simulation compared SCD with Erlang-C rules, an in-sample chance constraint and conformal bands on call counts, under a pre-specified plan.

Results. SCD met the target on 0.876–0.906 of test days, using 1–4% more unit-hours than a hindsight benchmark and 5–13% more than the Erlang-C rule MOL. The pre-specified validity criterion held in 4–5 of 7 areas (6 required). Erlang-C rules, which target an hourly delay probability, met the day-level target on 0.389–0.800 of days. Conformal bands on call counts cost 27–84% more than SCD. Coverage fell when demand grew after calibration (Spearman ρ = −0.57). In exploratory comparisons with current drift-adaptive conformal methods, an adaptive level reached 0.896–0.909 for at most 1.0% more unit-hours than SCD, recency weighting 0.906–0.937 for 1.1–3.2% more, and quantile tracking 0.898–0.938; a trend-adjusted load did not help.

Conclusions. Calibrating the staffing decision, not the demand forecast, gave near-nominal day-level reliability for a modest cost, but the pre-specified validity criterion failed under growing demand, where the calibration must adapt. The target concerns unit availability in a single pool, not response times.
