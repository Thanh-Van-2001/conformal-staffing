Background. Emergency medical service (EMS) staffing rules target an average hourly delay, not how often a day goes badly. Conformal prediction has bounded EMS demand forecasts but not the staffing decision itself.

Methods. We used 4,696,716 dispatch records from seven New York City and San Francisco areas (weekdays, 2022–2025). A day met the target if at most 5% of calls waited for a free unit. Split conformal calibration of the decision (SCD) set a square-root staffing plan’s safety factor to a rank of the factors recent days had needed. Trace-driven simulation over 287 out-of-sample 20-day windows compared SCD with Erlang-C rules, an in-sample chance constraint and conformal call-count bands, under a plan fixed in advance.

Results. SCD met the target on 0.876–0.906 of days (goal 0.90), for 5–13% more unit-hours than the Erlang-C rule MOL (0.445–0.800). The pre-specified validity criterion failed (4–5 of 7 areas; 6 required); the in-sample rule was not significantly less reliable. Coverage fell as demand grew (Spearman ρ = −0.57). Call-count bands cost 27–84% more. Exploratory recency weighting reached 0.906–0.937 for 1.1–3.2% more.

Conclusions. Calibrating the decision, not the forecast, gave near-nominal reliability at modest cost; under growing demand, calibration must adapt.
