The Editor
Prehospital Emergency Care

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "How many ambulances for nine good days in ten? A conformal staffing rule tested in seven EMS service areas", for publication as an original contribution.

EMS agencies set hourly ambulance staffing with queueing rules that aim at an average delay. They do not say how often a whole day will go badly. The manuscript tests a simple alternative on 4.7 million public dispatch records from New York City and San Francisco. For each recent day, the rule finds the smallest safety margin that would have kept that day within the service target. It then staffs with a rank of these margins chosen so that, in principle, nine days in ten meet the target.

Over more than three years of out-of-sample planning, the rule met the target on 88 to 91 percent of days. It used 1 to 4 percent more unit-hours than a hindsight benchmark and 5 to 13 percent more than the common Erlang-C rule. The Erlang-C rules met the day-level target on only 39 to 80 percent of days. Calibrating the demand forecast instead of the staffing decision cost 27 to 84 percent more unit-hours.

We report a negative pre-specified result with equal prominence. The validity criterion failed narrowly, because coverage fell when demand grew after calibration, and an adaptive level corrected most of the shortfall.

The analysis plan was fixed in version control before any staffing computation. Its amendments are disclosed, and the code and results are available at https://github.com/Thanh-Van-2001/conformal-staffing.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. The data were prepared for an earlier study by the same author on a different question, which is under review elsewhere; this is disclosed in the manuscript. The use of generative AI tools is also disclosed in the manuscript. The author declares no competing interests and received no specific funding.
